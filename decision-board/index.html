01 · Overview

What we're building and why

Tapestry Solutions answers large, technical government and defense RFPs (DoD, Boeing and other defense contractors), often with only a few weeks to respond. The knowledge needed for the answers already exists, but it is scattered, unversioned and hard to trust.

P1Knowledge is scattered

SharePoint, Confluence, past proposals and inboxes. Nobody knows which version is latest or approved.

P2Work gets repeated

Approved answers can't be found, so SMEs write them again from scratch.

P3Answers don't match

Proposals contradict each other, which is a credibility and compliance risk for defense customers.

P4SMEs are bottlenecks

The same questions go to the same few engineers on every bid.

P5Deadlines are unforgiving

A late submission can lose a multi-million-dollar contract outright.

P6There is no approval trail

Content gets reused on the basis of "I think this is right", with no record of approval.

The approach

Scattered sourcesTeam sites, wikis, past proposals, policies
→
Ingest & checkExtract, de-duplicate, record owner, version and classification
→
Trusted knowledge libraryApproved, current, access-controlled, audited
→
Proposal workflowUnderstand RFP → plan → find evidence → draft
→
People reviewSMEs fill gaps; reviewers approve
→
Editable exportAgreed template plus requirements checklist

Approved proposal text goes back into the library only after its owner reviews it. Drafts never become "knowledge" automatically.

The proposal journey

  1. 1UploadRFP and attachments. Unreadable files are rejected with a reason.
  2. 2UnderstandRequirements, deadlines and instructions, with page references. People can correct errors.
  3. 3PlanOutline and compliance matrix. Gaps and conflicts are flagged.
  4. 4Find evidenceApproved, current, permitted content only.
  5. 5DraftEvery claim linked to a source. Missing facts become questions.
  6. 6ReviewEdit, regenerate, approve. Versions are kept.
  7. 7ExportEditable document. A person submits it.

Promises the product keeps

  • Every material claim links to its source document and version.
  • Only approved, current content the user may see reaches a draft.
  • Nothing is invented. Pricing, staffing, commitments and certifications come from people.
  • People approve everything, and nothing is submitted automatically.
  • RFPs and source files are treated as untrusted input.
  • Every run is bounded by limits and can be audited.

02 · Tensions

Where the two documents disagree

These are the conflicts between the July evaluation and the September brief. Each one feeds a decision below.

C1

What the POC should prove

July evaluation

Lead with retrieval and grounded Q&A, not proposal generation. Small curated set, about four weeks.

29 Sept brief

Prove the complete workflow end to end, then add a bounded agent, an MCP adapter and a measured comparison.

Suggestion: Decide explicitly. A thin end-to-end slice keeps the proposal value visible without building everything at once. See D1.

C2

Build, buy or hybrid

July evaluation

Recommends a hybrid: a commercial or light workspace on top of a knowledge layer that Tapestry controls.

29 Sept brief

Assumes a fully custom build (Angular screens, Spring Boot workflow).

Suggestion: Record the decision and its reasons, because Tapestry will likely ask why we aren't using an existing RFP tool. See D2.

C3

"Confidence score" on answers

July evaluation

Every answer carries citations, approval status and a confidence score.

29 Sept brief

A model-based verifier gives a signal but can't certify accuracy or compliance.

Suggestion: Drop the numeric confidence score, which encourages over-trust. Show evidence indicators instead: cited or uncited, approval status, source age, conflicts found, and gaps.

C4

Writing approved drafts back to the library

July evaluation

SME-approved drafts are written back into the Knowledge Fabric so it improves with each proposal.

29 Sept brief

Keep generated drafts separate from approved knowledge. Reuse only after the owner approves.

Suggestion: Define a promotion flow: proposal text is nominated, then owner-reviewed, then becomes approved library content, with lineage kept. This connects to D9.

05 · Risks

Key risks and mitigations

Source access or security approval is slow and blocks the POCHighHighPublic or synthetic RFPs and exported documents. Request access in week 1. Controlled import before live connectors (D7).
CUI is confirmed late and forces re-platformingMediumHighPick services that can run inside a compliant boundary. Keep the model and embedding provider swappable (D3, D11).
Retrieval quality on long technical documents is poorMediumHighHybrid keyword + semantic search, structure-aware chunking, golden-set evaluation, fixed-pipeline baseline first.

06 · Questions for Tapestry

What we need Tapestry to tell us

Combined from both documents. Several decisions above can't be closed until these are answered.

Outcome & scope

  1. What output is expected: a compliance matrix, section drafts, a full proposal or all three? Which template and formats?
  2. Which RFP types and formats matter first (scanned PDFs, tables, appendices, amendments)?
  3. What counts as success for leadership: time saved, more bids, win rate or SME load?
  4. How many RFPs a quarter, with what turnaround? How much time goes on searching versus writing?
  5. Has a deadline been missed or nearly missed because of content problems?

Content & ownership

  1. Which repositories hold the most valuable reusable content, and who owns each?
  2. Who decides approval, validity, classification and access? Is there a sign-off process already?
  3. Which facts are reusable, and which must be supplied per pursuit (pricing, staffing, commitments)?
  4. Who resolves duplicates, contradictions and missing metadata, now and after go-live?
  5. Is there a curated answer library or boilerplate set today?

Security & access

  1. Is any content ITAR-controlled or CUI? What is the classification scheme?
  2. Which hosting and model-provider boundaries are allowed (commercial, FedRAMP High / IL4–5, GovCloud, on-prem)?
  3. Who signs off security for the POC, and how long does it take?
  4. What access is possible per source: connector/API, export or manual import?
  5. Which identity provider is in use?

Process

  1. Who reviews technical, legal, security and commercial claims? What are the review stages and turnaround?
  2. Can we have 1–3 permitted past RFPs with their final proposals, for the baseline and the golden set?
